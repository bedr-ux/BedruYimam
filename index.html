Hi there, I’m Bedru! 👋

I am a Lecturer and Researcher in the Department of Information Technology at Wollo University (Kombolcha Institute of Technology - KIoT) in Ethiopia. I hold a Master of Science (MSc) in Information Technology from Bahir Dar University.

Language is the bedrock of culture, identity, and thought. Today, over 100 million people speak Ethiopian Semitic languages like **Amharic **, yet in the era of modern Large Language Models, these rich Ge’ez-script languages remain vastly underserved. They face massive computational penalties (“Token Tax”), heavy token fragmentation, and a lack of reliable clinical or educational tools.

My mission is to help close this digital language divide: building language and speech technologies that are culturally grounded, compute-efficient, and practically useful to everyday communities.

What I Work On & Why It Matters

The "Token Tax"

Why should an Amharic speaker pay nearly 10× more compute cost than an English speaker for the exact same prompt? We benchmark tokenizers and diagnose byte-fallback collapse so modern foundation models treat Ge'ez script equitably.

Democratizing AI on Modest Hardware

True democratization means researchers and developers in Africa can fine-tune high-quality models on a single GPU. We adapt open-weight models (Qwen, Gemma, Aya) to Ethiopian languages using Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT / LoRA).

AI for Healthcare & Real Needs

Bringing reliable AI to clinics with spotty internet connectivity. We build offline-capable Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) assistants for maternal and antenatal healthcare (ANC), providing rural health workers with trusted guidance in their native language.

Speech AI for Everyday Voices

Language is spoken before it is written. We adapt foundation speech models (Whisper) to regional Ethiopian dialects—specifically capturing the acoustic and phonetic nuances of Wollo Amharic that standard models ignore.

In the Classroom & Community 🎓

Teaching is one of the most fulfilling dimensions of my academic journey. At Wollo University, I work closely with undergraduate students teaching courses.

I strongly believe that the future of African language technologies must be shaped by African students and researchers solving local problems from the ground up. In the classroom and lab, I focus on:

  • Helping students move from theoretical equations to building functioning, open-source tools.
  • Community Datasets: Championing participatory, grassroots data collection through communities like Masakhane.
  • Resourcefulness: Inspiring students to innovate creatively, proving that high-impact research can happen even with modest compute budgets.

Beyond the Terminal ☕

When I’m not inspecting token distributions or training LoRA adapters, you’ll likely find me:

    Sukairaj Hafiz Imam, Babangida Sani, Dawit Ketema Gete, and 6 more authors
    In Proceedings of the Sixth Workshop on African Natural Language Processing (AfricaNLP 2025), Jul 2025
    Abs HTML PDF

    Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) technologies have transformed human-computer interaction; however, low-resource languages in Africa remain significantly underrepresented in both research and practical applications. This study investigates the major challenges hindering the development of ASR systems for these languages, including data scarcity, linguistic complexity, limited computational resources, acoustic variability, and ethical concerns surrounding bias and privacy. The findings highlight the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration and sustained investment to advance low-resource speech technology.
